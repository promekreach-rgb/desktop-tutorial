03 Plagiarism Detection Tool Performance Analysis

Source: Leong & Zhang (2025). Analysis conducted across 200 content samples including 50 direct copies, 75 paraphrased versions, and 75 AI-generated texts. Three tools evaluated: Turnitin (keyword-matching), Grammarly (hybrid AI + rules), and Copyleaks (deep learning + NLP).

Methodological Note: Detection rates are expressed as percentages of correctly identified plagiarised content per content category. Speed is measured as average report generation time in seconds. False positive rates reflect the percentage of legitimate work incorrectly flagged as plagiarised. Sample sizes: n = 200 (accuracy/speed), n = 50 AI texts (AI detection), n = 150 surveys (user satisfaction).

07 Synthesised Research Insights

The following insights are derived through cross-source synthesis of all five studies and represent the most critical, evidence-backed conclusions relevant to the thesis that AI strengthens academic integrity.

INSIGHT 01
OR = 0.911
AI Literacy is the Strongest Protective Factor
Students with greater knowledge of AI tools show significantly lower plagiarism odds (p<0.001). Digital literacy programmes represent the most evidence-backed institutional intervention. — Torres-Diaz et al. (2025)
INSIGHT 02
3× Gap
Deep Learning Outperforms Traditional Detection
Copyleaks detects 75% of AI-generated content vs. Turnitin's 25% — a threefold difference that directly supports investment in AI-powered detection tools. — Leong & Zhang (2025)
INSIGHT 03
52%
Complex Paraphrasing Remains a Critical Gap
Even the most advanced AI tools detect only 52% of complex paraphrased content — the most common student evasion strategy — necessitating continuous R&D and human oversight. — Leong & Zhang (2025)
INSIGHT 04
95.5%
Bans Are Ineffective — Education Is Essential
Only 4.5% of students have never used AI tools academically. Prohibitive approaches are therefore impractical; ethical AI education is the only viable long-term strategy. — Torres-Diaz et al. (2025)
INSIGHT 05
↑ Fastest
AI Bypassers: The Emerging Frontier Threat
AI humaniser tools that deliberately alter AI-generated text to evade detection are now the fastest-growing form of academic misconduct, prompting Turnitin to deploy dedicated bypass detection. — Chechitelli / Turnitin (2025)
INSIGHT 06
5 of 5
Institutional Policy: The Universal Recommendation
All five sources independently conclude that clear, explicit AI use policies are the single most critical institutional action needed to strengthen academic integrity in higher education.
